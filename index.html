Read the land. Arrange the room. Choose the time.

Feng Shui is the Chinese art of placing a building, a door, a bed and a desk so that the space supports the people inside it. Here is how a master actually does it — the instruments, the formulas, and the history behind them.

Why it matters

藏风聚气“Shelter from the wind, gather the qi.” The idea goes back to the Book of Burial attributed to Guo Pu (276–324 CE): qi scatters when it rides the wind and stops where it meets water. That single sentence gave the art its name — 风 wind, 水 water.

  • 门
    The door is the mouth of the home

    Where the main door faces decides which energies come in. A master measures it to the degree, because one sector over can change the whole chart.

  • 人
    The same flat suits people differently

    Each person's birth data gives them favourable directions and elements. Partners in a business are read together so no one is placed against their own luck.

  • 时
    Spaces change with time

    Energy in each sector shifts every 20-year period, every year and every month. A good layout in 2004 can need adjusting in 2026 — we are now in Period 9, which began in February 2024.

  • 形
    Good design and good Feng Shui overlap

    Light, airflow, a solid wall behind your seat, a clear entrance, no sharp corners at eye level. Much of classical advice is simply good architecture written down 1,700 years ago.

Luo Pan罗盘

The Feng Shui compass

A magnetic needle set in a disc of rings — from a handful to more than forty — each ring a different formula: the eight trigrams, the 24 Mountains, the 60 dragons, the stars. The square base and two red threads let the master sight a wall or a door exactly.

How it's used
Stand inside the main door, look straight out, keep the plate level and away from metal frames. Turn the dial until the needle sits on the north–south line, then read where the red thread crosses the 24 Mountains ring.
Why it matters
Each of the 24 Mountains spans only 15°. Being 3° out can put a home in the wrong chart, so every other calculation starts here.
02 / 09

Form School峦头

Reading the land and the street

The oldest layer. The ideal site sits like a person in an armchair: higher ground or a taller building behind (Black Tortoise), slightly higher support on the left (Green Dragon), lower on the right (White Tiger), and an open, bright space in front (Red Phoenix).

What we check
Roads pointing at the door, sharp building corners, gaps between towers, lifts or stairs facing the door, water views, the shape of the unit.
Why it matters
Form overrides formula. A perfect compass chart cannot fix a road aimed at the front door — so the master walks the site first.
03 / 09

Flying Stars玄空飞星

Xuan Kong — space and time together

The numbers 1 to 9 "fly" through the nine squares of the Lo Shu grid in a fixed path. Which number lands where depends on the facing direction and the 20-year period the building belongs to. Each square ends up with a mountain star (people, health) and a facing star (wealth).

How it's used
Lay the grid over the floor plan from its centre point. Put active rooms — the door, the living room, the office — where the good stars fall; put stores and toilets on the difficult ones; cure the rest with the right element.
Why it matters
It explains why two identical flats in the same block can feel different, and why a home that did well for twenty years can change. In Period 9 (2024–2043), the 9 star is the prosperous one.
04 / 09

Eight Mansions八宅

Your personal directions

Your birth year and gender give a Kua number from 1 to 9. It sorts the eight compass directions into four that support you — wealth, health, relationships, stability — and four that drain you. People fall into an East group or a West group, and houses do too.

How it's used
Face your desk toward your wealth direction, point the head of the bed to your health or relationship direction, and avoid sitting toward your "total loss" direction.
Why it matters
It is personal. In a family or a partnership, each person gets their own seat and direction instead of one rule for everyone.
05 / 09

Ba Zi八字

The Four Pillars of Destiny

Your year, month, day and hour of birth are each written as two characters — a Heavenly Stem and an Earthly Branch — giving eight characters in all. Together they show the balance of the Five Elements you were born with, and which elements you need more of.

What you give us
Your ordinary English birthday, birth time and birthplace. We convert it using the solar calendar — the Ba Zi month changes at solar terms like 立春 (around 4 February), not at Chinese New Year — and correct for true solar time.
Why it matters
It decides your colours, materials and the elements to add to a room. It also shows good and difficult years through the 10-year luck pillars, which helps time a move or a renovation.
06 / 09

Five Elements五行

The language of every cure

Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water feed and control each other in two cycles. Every star, direction and person maps onto an element — so every remedy is an element too.

In a room
Wood plants, timber, green · Fire lighting, red · Earth stone, ceramics, beige · Metal brass, round shapes, white · Water glass, water features, navy.
Why it matters
This is how a calculation becomes a design brief. A brass six-rod chime in one corner, a red rug in another — each is an element placed for a reason, not decoration.
07 / 09

Lu Ban ruler鲁班尺

The carpenter's lucky measure

Named after Lu Ban, the master craftsman of the Spring and Autumn period, honoured as the patron of Chinese carpenters. A 42.9 cm cycle is divided into eight sections — four auspicious (Wealth 财, Righteous 义, Officer 官, Origin 本) and four to avoid (Sickness 病, Separation 离, Robbery 劫, Harm 害).

How it's used
Measure door openings, desks, beds, cabinets and altars. If a size falls in a black section, adjust it by a few centimetres into a red one. Altars use the related Ding Lan 丁兰 ruler.
Why it matters
It costs nothing at the drawing stage. Getting the main door and the director's desk into a good section is one of the easiest wins in a renovation.
08 / 09

Tong Shu通书

The Chinese almanac — choosing the date

Once compiled by the imperial astronomical bureau, the Tong Shu (通胜 Tung Sing in Cantonese) gives each day a stem-branch pair, one of twelve "day officers", and the animal it clashes with. It is the oldest continuously printed almanac in the world.

How it's used
Pick days marked Success 成, Open 开 or Stable 定 for a grand opening, moving in, signing or starting renovation — and drop any day that clashes with the owner's or partners' birth animal.
Why it matters
A good layout started on a clashing day is a mixed start. Timing is the 天时 part of the reading.

From chart to design布局

Furniture, colour, material, shape

The last step turns numbers into a room you want to live or work in. Master Pan reads your floor plan and your designer's mock-ups, then marks what to move, what to change and why.

Typical advice
Command position for the bed and the boss's desk — solid wall behind, diagonal view of the door. Round or soft-cornered furniture. No mirror facing the door or the bed. Break a straight line from the front door to a back window. Plants with round leaves, not spikes, near the entrance.
Why it matters
It is where the reading pays off. Changes are usually small — a desk turned, a colour changed, a console added — and cheapest before the carpenter starts.
History

Six thousand years of reading the land

Feng Shui grew alongside Chinese astronomy, architecture and the compass itself. These are the milestones behind the tools above.

  1. c. 4000 BCE
    Dragon and Tiger at Xishuipo 西水坡

    A Neolithic grave in Puyang, Henan, lays out a dragon and a tiger in shells on either side of the body — east and west — the same pairing used in Form School today.

  2. 5th c. BCE
    Lu Ban 鲁班

    The legendary carpenter of the state of Lu. Builders later named their measuring rules and their handbook, the Ming-dynasty Lu Ban Jing, after him.

  3. Han dynasty
    The diviner's board 式盘

    A square earth plate and a round heaven plate marked with directions and stars — the ancestor of the Luo Pan's rings.

  4. 276–324 CE
    Guo Pu 郭璞 and the Book of Burial

    The text attributed to him explains that qi scatters with wind and is held by water — and gives Feng Shui its name.

  5. 9th c.
    Yang Yunsong 杨筠松

    Tang-dynasty master remembered as the founder of the Form School, nicknamed "Yang who saves the poor".

  6. 1088
    The magnetic needle

    Song-dynasty scholar Shen Kuo 沈括 describes a magnetised needle compass and magnetic declination in his Dream Pool Essays. The needle becomes the heart of the Luo Pan.

  7. Song dynasty
    Four Pillars take shape

    Ba Zi reading is systematised around the Day Master, a method credited to Xu Ziping 徐子平 and still called Zi Ping 子平 today.

  8. 17th c.
    Jiang Dahong 蒋大鸿

    Ming–Qing master who championed the time-based Xuan Kong 玄空 school. The Qing era also produced the Eight Mansions classic Ba Zhai Ming Jing 八宅明镜.

  9. Early 1900s
    Shen's Xuan Kong 沈氏玄空学

    The notes of Shen Zhurong 沈竹礽 are published and make Flying Star charts teachable. Modern practice largely follows this text.

  10. 4 Feb 2024
    Period 9 begins 九运

    The 20-year cycle turns to the 9 star (Fire), running to 2043. Charts, colours and enhancers are recalculated for the new period.

Real stories

When the plan was changed

Documented cases where Feng Shui advice changed a real building or attraction. Each one links to its source.

Singapore Flyer

Marina Bay, Singapore · 2008
Before
The giant observation wheel opened in 2008 turning anti-clockwise, as seen from Marina Centre.
After
On 4 August 2008, a few months after opening, the direction of rotation was reversed on the advice of Feng Shui masters.

Source: Wikipedia — Singapore Flyer

Suntec City

Singapore · 1990s
Design
Geomancy masters advised on the layout. The towers are arranged like a left hand: the 18-storey tower is the thumb, four 45-storey towers the fingers, the convention centre the wrist.
Feature
The Fountain of Wealth sits in the palm like a ring. It was listed as the world's largest fountain in the 1998 Guinness Book of Records, and visitors still walk round it touching the water for luck.

Source: National Library Board Infopedia — Suntec City

Hong Kong Disneyland

Lantau Island, Hong Kong · 2005
Before
The park brought in a Feng Shui consultant while it was being designed.
After
On that advice the entrance was shifted by 12 degrees, so that qi would not flow straight out of the park.

Source: Mental Floss — Hong Kong's architecture and Feng Shui

HSBC Main Building

Central, Hong Kong · 1985
Advice
The bank's two bronze guardian lions, Stephen and Stitt, were placed in front of the new Norman Foster tower only after extensive consultation with Feng Shui practitioners.
Myth
The rooftop "cannons" said to be aimed at the Bank of China Tower are maintenance cranes that were in the original plans — a good reminder to check stories.

Source: Wikipedia — HSBC Building (Hong Kong)

MGM Grand lion

Las Vegas, USA · 1998
Before
A huge gold lion's head framed the main entrance. Many Chinese guests saw walking in under a lion's open mouth as bad luck, and some refused to use that entrance.
After
In 1998 the lion head was replaced with the seated lion statue that stands there today. The company cited cultural sensitivity (and, its spokesman added, that it was ugly).

Source: Casino.org — the MGM Grand lion

"Dragon gates"

Hong Kong · since the 1980s
Belief
Dragons are said to live in the hills behind the city and travel down to the harbour to drink. A tower that blocks the way blocks the qi.
Built
Several Hong Kong residential towers were designed with a large opening through the middle — a "dragon gate" — so the path from mountain to water stays open.

Source: Mental Floss — Hong Kong's architecture and Feng Shui

These stories show how seriously Feng Shui is taken in real projects. They record what was changed and why; they do not prove that a change caused later success. Feng Shui is a traditional practice, best used together with good design, light and ventilation.

Questions

Good to know

Do I need my Chinese (lunar) birthday?

No. Give your normal English birthday. Ba Zi uses the solar calendar, so it is converted from the English date. If you already have your Ba Zi written down, add it too.

What if I don't know my birth time?

Tick the box on the form. The year, month and day pillars still give your Kua number and most of your element balance. The hour pillar adds detail, so check your birth certificate if you can.

Why does the floor plan need to be a PDF?

A clean architect's or developer's plan shows walls, doors and windows to scale, which is what the Flying Star grid is laid over. A clear photo works if you don't have a PDF.

Does Feng Shui mean expensive renovation?

Usually not. Most advice is placement, colour, a few objects and timing. It is cheapest when Master Pan sees the plan before the carpenter starts.

Is Feng Shui scientific?

It is a traditional practice, not a science, and its effects are not scientifically proven. Many of its rules — light, ventilation, a supported seat, a calm entrance — are also good design.
